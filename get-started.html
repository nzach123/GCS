Chapter One

Technology Stack & Architecture

There are many pro and cons to each engine. Decide on what feature and mechanics you want to implement first then decide on which game best fits for you.

Unity

Best for: Cross-platform Commercial Releases, Mobile, VR/AR. The industry standard for versatility. We leverage Unity's robust C# environment to build scalable systems that deploy seamlessly across console, PC, and mobile.

Our Focus: Custom render pipelines, performance optimization for constrained hardware, and tool development.

Unreal Engine 5

Best for: High-Fidelity 3D, Console Focus, Large-Scale Worlds. When visual fidelity and AAA pipelines are non-negotiable. We utilize C++ and Blueprints to maximize the potential of Nanite and Lumen without sacrificing framerate.

Our Focus: Advanced lighting, replication for multiplayer, and cinematic workflow integration.

Godot

Best for: Lightweight Applications, 2D, Open Source Flexibility. A powerful, license-free alternative for projects requiring total code ownership. Godot eliminates royalty fees and offers a lean architecture ideal for rapid iteration and specialized hardware.

Our Focus: GDScript/C++ modules, custom engine modifications, and lean executable targets.

Rapid Prototyping & Retro Systems

For game jams, concept validation, or highly stylized retro projects, we deploy specialized engines to cut development time significantly.

  • Construct 3: Logic-heavy 2D prototypes and HTML5 delivery.
  • Pico-8: Authentic 8-bit constraints and fantasy console development.
  • GB Studio: Native Game Boy ROM creation and handheld hardware compatibility.
  • RPG Maker: Narrative-focused prototyping and classic JRPG systems.
