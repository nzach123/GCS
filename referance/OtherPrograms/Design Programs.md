### Art Production (Visuals & Polish)

Great games need great visuals. Whether you're creating stylized characters, pixel art masterpieces, or photorealistic environments, these tools are the weapons of choice.

#### 3D Modeling & Sculpting

**Blender** `Free` `Industry Standard`
The undisputed champion of free 3D software. Major studios use Blender for everything from modeling to animation, rigging, and visual effects. It is a complete pipeline in a single program.
**[Download Blender](https://www.blender.org/download/)**

**Autodesk Maya** `Paid` `Industry Standard`
If you want to work at a AAA studio, you need to know Maya. It is the absolute gold standard for 3D animation and rigging. While expensive, its toolset for character animation is unmatched.
**[Get Maya](https://www.autodesk.com/products/maya/overview)**

**Maxon ZBrush** `Paid` `Specialist`
The industry king of digital sculpting. From movie monsters to highly detailed game characters, ZBrush handles high-poly geometry better than anything else. It feels more like sculpting clay than working with software.
**[Get ZBrush](https://www.maxon.net/en/zbrush)**

**MagicaVoxel** `Free`
If you are aiming for that charming, blocky voxel aesthetic (think *Crossy Road* or *Minecraft*-adjacent styles), this is the tool. It is lightweight, includes a powerful built-in renderer, and is incredibly easy to pick up.
**[Download MagicaVoxel](https://ephtracy.github.io/)**

#### 2D Art & Concept

**Adobe Photoshop** `Paid (Subscription)` `Industry Standard`
The tool that defined the medium. Photoshop remains the most versatile and widely used raster graphics editor in the professional world. Its ecosystem of brushes, plugins, and tutorials is infinite.
**[Get Photoshop](https://www.adobe.com/products/photoshop.html)**

**Krita** `Free`
The best open-source alternative to Photoshop for digital painting. Its brush engine, layer management, and color tools are top-tier, making it ideal for concept art, hand-painted textures, and illustrations.
**[Download Krita](https://krita.org/en/download/krita-desktop/)**

**Inkscape** `Free`
For crisp, scalable vector graphics. If your game needs clean UI icons, vector-based character assets, or resolution-independent logos, Inkscape is the open-source answer to Illustrator.
**[Download Inkscape](https://inkscape.org/)**

#### Pixel Art & 2D Animation

**Aseprite** `Paid` `Source Available`
The world's premiere pixel art tool. If you're making a retro-styled game, Aseprite's timeline-based workflow, onion skinning, and tilemap support make animation intuitive and fast.
**[Buy on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/431730/Aseprite/)**

**Spine 2D** `Paid` `Professional`
The industry standard for 2D skeletal animation. If you see fluid, complex 2D animation in a game (like *Hollow Knight* or *Slay the Spire*), it was likely made with Spine. It allows for mesh deformation and incredibly smooth motion.
**[Get Spine](http://esotericsoftware.com/)**

#### Texturing & Materials

**Adobe Substance 3D Painter** `Paid` `Industry Standard`
"Photoshop for 3D models." This is how the vast majority of professional game textures are made today. You paint materials directly onto your 3D model with incredible realism and detail.
**[Get Substance 3D Painter](https://www.adobe.com/products/substance3d-painter.html)**

**Material Maker** `Free`
A powerful procedural texture generation tool based on the Godot Engine. It allows you to create complex, PBR-ready materials using a node-based workflow similar to Substance Designer, but completely free.
**[Download Material Maker](https://www.materialmaker.org/)**

### UI & Experience Design

User Interface (UI) is how players interact with your game. Don't leave it until the last minute.

**Figma** `Free Tier` `Web-based`
The industry standard for UI/UX prototyping. Map out your game's menus, HUDs, and user flows before writing a single line of code. Its collaborative features are excellent for teams.
**[Try Figma](https://www.figma.com/)**

### Level Design

Building the world your players will inhabit.

**LDtk (Level Designer Toolkit)** `Free`
A modern 2D level editor created by the developer of *Dead Cells*. It focuses on user-friendliness and strong typing for level data, making it a favorite for Haxe and generic 2D engine users.
**[Download LDtk](https://ldtk.io/)**

**Tiled** `Free`
A generic, open-source tile map editor. Tiled is incredibly versatile for 2D level design, supporting orthogonal, isometric, and hexagonal maps. It exports to almost every game engine.
**[Download Tiled](https://www.mapeditor.org/)**
